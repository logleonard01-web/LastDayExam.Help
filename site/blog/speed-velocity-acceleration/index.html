Speed, Velocity and Acceleration

Supporting explainer · Science · 1 min read

The definitions, formulas and typical calculations for motion in a straight line.

On this page

  • Acceleration
  • Equations of motion at constant acceleration
  • Distance–time and velocity–time graphs
  • Common mistakes
  • Quick recap

Speed and velocity

Speed is how fast something moves, regardless of direction. Velocity is speed in a given direction, so velocity is a vector and speed is a scalar.

speed = distance ÷ time

Units: metres per second (m/s) or kilometres per hour (km/h).

Example. A cyclist travels 120 m in 20 s. Speed = 120 ÷ 20 = 6 m/s.

To convert m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6. So 6 m/s = 21.6 km/h.

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity:

a = (v − u) ÷ t

where u is the initial velocity, v the final velocity and t the time.

Example. A car speeds up from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 8 s. a = (25 − 5) ÷ 8 = 2.5 m/s².

A negative acceleration means slowing down (deceleration), if the object is moving in the positive direction.

Equations of motion at constant acceleration

  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as

Example. A ball is dropped from rest. Taking g = 9.8 m/s², find its speed after 3 s.

  • v = u + at = 0 + 9.8 × 3 = 29.4 m/s.

Distance–time and velocity–time graphs

  • On a distance–time graph, the gradient is speed. A horizontal line means stationary.
  • On a velocity–time graph, the gradient is acceleration and the area under the graph is distance travelled.

Common mistakes

  • Using minutes with metres per second without converting.
  • Confusing distance (total path length) with displacement (straight-line distance in a direction).
  • Saying a negative acceleration always means "slowing down". It depends on the direction of motion.

Quick recap

Speed = distance ÷ time; acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time. Use gradients and areas on graphs.

Key vocabulary

  • VelocitySpeed in a given direction.
  • AccelerationThe rate of change of velocity.
  • DisplacementThe straight-line distance and direction from the start to the end point.
