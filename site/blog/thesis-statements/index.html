Writing a Strong Thesis Statement

Supporting explainer · English · 1 min read

What a thesis statement is, what makes one strong, and how to improve a weak one.

On this page
  • What makes a thesis strong
  • Weak and strong examples
  • A formula to start with
  • Revise as you write
  • Quick recap

A thesis statement is a sentence or two that states the central claim of your essay. It is your answer to the question, which the rest of the essay supports.

What makes a thesis strong

  • Arguable: a reasonable person could disagree.
  • Specific: it names exactly what you are claiming.
  • Focused: it can be developed in the length of the essay.
  • Answers the question if there is one.

Weak and strong examples

WeakWhyStronger
"Pollution is bad."Too general; nobody disagrees."Local governments should limit private car use in city centres, because doing so reduces air pollution more cost-effectively than increasing public transport capacity alone."
"This essay is about Macbeth."An announcement, not a claim."In Macbeth, ambition becomes destructive because it replaces moral restraint with fear."
"The Industrial Revolution had many effects."Vague."The Industrial Revolution transformed Britain's economy mainly by shifting work from home to factories, which changed family and urban life."

A formula to start with

Topic + claim + because/by reasoning. For example: "Remote work improves productivity for many employees because it reduces commuting time and allows focused work."

A formula is a starting point. Revise it until it sounds natural.

Revise as you write

Your first thesis is a working draft. If your evidence leads to a different conclusion, update the thesis so it matches the essay.

Quick recap

State an arguable, specific, focused claim that answers the question. Revise it to match your evidence.

Key vocabulary

  • Thesis statementA sentence that states the central, arguable claim of an essay.
  • ArgumentA claim supported by reasons and evidence.
