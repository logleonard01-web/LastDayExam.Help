Probability Basics

Supporting explainer · Mathematics · 1 min read

How to calculate simple probabilities, including combined events, with worked examples.

On this page

  • Complementary events
  • Independent events
  • Mutually exclusive events
  • Without replacement

  • Quick recap

Probability measures how likely an event is, on a scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). It can be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage.

The basic formula

When all outcomes are equally likely:

P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes.

Example. A fair six-sided die is rolled. The probability of rolling an even number is 3/6 = 1/2, because 2, 4 and 6 are favourable out of six outcomes.

Complementary events

P(not A) = 1 − P(A).

If the probability of rain is 0.3, the probability of no rain is 0.7.

Independent events

Two events are independent if one does not affect the other. For independent events:

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

Example. Toss a fair coin and roll a fair die. P(heads and a 6) = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.

Mutually exclusive events

Events that cannot happen together are mutually exclusive. Then:

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).

Example. Roll a die. P(1 or 2) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.

Without replacement

A bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. Two are drawn without putting the first back. P(both red):

  • First red: 3/5.
  • Second red: 2/4 = 1/2.
  • Both: 3/5 × 1/2 = 3/10.

Common mistakes

  • Adding probabilities for events that are not mutually exclusive.
  • Forgetting that the total changes when items are not replaced.
  • Giving a probability greater than 1 or less than 0.
  • Thinking that after several heads, tails is "due". For a fair coin, each toss is independent.

Quick recap

Count favourable over total, use 1 − P(A) for "not", multiply for independent "and", add for mutually exclusive "or".

Key vocabulary

  • ProbabilityA number from 0 to 1 that measures how likely an event is.
  • OutcomeOne possible result of a probability experiment.
  • EventA set of one or more outcomes in probability.
