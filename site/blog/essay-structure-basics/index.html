Essay Structure Basics

Cornerstone guide · English · 2 min read

How to organise an essay with an introduction, focused paragraphs and a conclusion.

On this page

  • The introduction
  • Body paragraphs
  • Transitions
  • The conclusion
  • Common mistakes
  • Quick recap

A clear essay structure helps the reader follow your reasoning. Most academic essays follow the same basic shape.

The three parts

  1. Introduction: introduces the topic, gives necessary context, and states your thesis statement.
  2. Body: a series of paragraphs, each developing one point that supports the thesis.
  3. Conclusion: restates the main argument in light of the evidence and explains why it matters.

The introduction

A strong introduction usually does three things:

  • Opens with context or a focused statement about the topic.
  • Narrows to the specific question.
  • Ends with a thesis that answers the question.

Avoid long background passages and avoid announcing "In this essay I will..." if a more direct statement is possible.

Body paragraphs

A common pattern is PEEL:

  • Point: a topic sentence stating the paragraph's main idea.
  • Evidence: a quotation, fact, example or data.
  • Explanation: how the evidence supports the point.
  • Link: how this connects back to the thesis or leads to the next point.

Example for a paragraph about a novel. The point: the setting reflects the character's isolation. The evidence: a short quotation describing an empty house. The explanation: how the description conveys loneliness. The link: how this supports the thesis about isolation.

Transitions

Use words and phrases that show relationships: however (contrast), therefore (result), for example (illustration), in addition (continuation), similarly (comparison).

The conclusion

Summarise the reasoning, not just the points. Say what follows from your argument. Do not introduce a major new point.

Common mistakes

  • A thesis that is a fact rather than an arguable claim.
  • Paragraphs with several unrelated points.
  • Evidence with no explanation.
  • A conclusion that repeats the introduction word for word.

Quick recap

Introduction with a thesis, one point per paragraph with evidence and explanation, linking words between ideas, and a conclusion that answers the question.

Key vocabulary

  • Thesis statementA sentence that states the central, arguable claim of an essay.
  • Topic sentenceA sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph.
  • ArgumentA claim supported by reasons and evidence.
  • EvidenceFacts, data, examples or sources used to support a claim.
