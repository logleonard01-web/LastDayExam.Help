Balancing Chemical Equations

Worked example · Science · 2 min read

A step-by-step method for balancing equations, with four worked examples.

  • Example 1: water
  • Example 2: methane burning
  • Example 3: magnesium and oxygen
  • Example 4: aluminium and oxygen
  • State symbols
  • Common mistakes

A balanced chemical equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides, because atoms are not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. This is the law of conservation of mass.

The method

  1. Write the correct formulas for reactants and products. Never change a formula to balance it.
  2. Count atoms of each element on each side.
  3. Adjust the numbers in front of formulas (coefficients) to balance one element at a time.
  4. Recount everything.
  5. If useful, balance elements that appear in only one compound on each side first, and leave hydrogen and oxygen for last.

Example 1: water

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

  • Left: 2 H, 2 O. Right: 2 H, 1 O.
  • Put 2 in front of H₂O: H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Now right has 4 H, 2 O.
  • Put 2 in front of H₂: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
  • Check: 4 H and 2 O on each side. ✓

Example 2: methane burning

CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

  • Carbon is balanced (1 each side).
  • Hydrogen: 4 on left, so put 2 in front of H₂O: CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
  • Oxygen on right: 2 + 2 = 4, so put 2 in front of O₂.
  • CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. ✓

Example 3: magnesium and oxygen

Mg + O₂ → MgO

  • Oxygen: 2 on left, 1 on right. Put 2 in front of MgO: Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.
  • Magnesium: 2 on right, so put 2 in front of Mg.
  • 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. ✓

Example 4: aluminium and oxygen

Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃

  • Oxygen: 2 on left, 3 on right. The lowest common multiple of 2 and 3 is 6.
  • 3O₂ gives 6 O on left, and 2Al₂O₃ gives 6 O on right.
  • Right has 4 Al, so put 4 in front of Al.
  • 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃. ✓

State symbols

Equations often include (s) for solid, (l) for liquid, (g) for gas and (aq) for aqueous solution.

Common mistakes

  • Changing subscripts, which changes the substance.
  • Balancing one element and forgetting to recheck the others.
  • Leaving a fractional coefficient when a whole number version is required. Multiply through to clear fractions.

Quick recap

Count atoms, adjust coefficients only, recheck. Never alter chemical formulas.

Key vocabulary

  • Chemical equationA representation of a chemical reaction using formulas.
  • ReactantA substance that is present at the start of a chemical reaction and is changed by it.
  • ProductA substance formed in a chemical reaction.
  • CoefficientA number that multiplies a variable.
