Fractions, Decimals and Percentages Compared

Supporting explainer · Mathematics · 1 min read

How the three forms relate, how to convert between them, and how to compare values.

On this page
  • Converting between forms
  • Useful equivalents to know
  • Finding a percentage of an amount
  • Percentage change
  • Comparing values
  • Common mistakes
  • Quick recap

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways to write the same kind of quantity: part of a whole.

Converting between forms

FromToMethodExample
FractionDecimalDivide numerator by denominator3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
DecimalPercentageMultiply by 1000.75 → 75%
PercentageDecimalDivide by 10040% → 0.40
PercentageFractionWrite over 100, then simplify40% = 40/100 = 2/5
DecimalFractionUse place value, then simplify0.6 = 6/10 = 3/5

Useful equivalents to know

  • 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%
  • 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
  • 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
  • 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%
  • 1/10 = 0.1 = 10%
  • 1/3 = 0.333... (repeating) ≈ 33.3%

Finding a percentage of an amount

To find 15% of 60: 10% of 60 is 6, 5% is 3, so 15% is 9. Or multiply: 0.15 × 60 = 9.

Percentage change

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Example. A price rises from 80 to 92.

  • Change: 92 − 80 = 12.
  • 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15.
  • Increase of 15%.

Comparing values

To compare 3/8, 0.4 and 35%, convert all to decimals: 3/8 = 0.375, 0.4, and 35% = 0.35. In order from smallest: 35%, 3/8, 0.4.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing the wrong way round when converting a fraction (use numerator ÷ denominator).
  • Using the new value instead of the original in percentage change.
  • Adding percentages from different bases. A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall does not return to the start: 100 → 110 → 99.

Quick recap

Think of the three forms as interchangeable. Memorise common equivalents, and for percentage change always divide by the original value.

Key vocabulary

  • FractionA number that represents part of a whole, written as one number over another.
  • RatioA comparison of two quantities showing how many times one contains the other.
  • PercentageA fraction expressed out of 100.
