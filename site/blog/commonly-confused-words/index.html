Commonly Confused Words

Vocabulary reference · English · 1 min read

Pairs of words that are often mixed up, with the difference and an example for each.

On this page
  • affect and effect
  • their, there, they're
  • its and it's
  • then and than
  • your and you're
  • principal and principle
  • accept and except
  • fewer and less
  • Strategy for exams

These pairs cause many errors in writing. Learn the difference for each.

affect and effect

  • Affect (usually a verb): to influence. The weather affected the match.
  • Effect (usually a noun): a result. The effect of the weather was a delay.
  • A quick test: if you can put "the" before the word, it is probably effect. (Both words have less common uses, such as "effect change", meaning to bring it about.)

their, there, they're

  • Their: belonging to them. Their books are on the table.
  • There: a place or the existence of something. The books are over there. There is a problem.
  • They're: they are. They're late.

its and it's

  • Its: belonging to it. The dog wagged its tail.
  • It's: it is or it has. It's raining.

then and than

  • Then: at that time, or next. We ate, then we left.
  • Than: used in comparisons. She is taller than her brother.

your and you're

  • Your: belonging to you.
  • You're: you are.

principal and principle

  • Principal: a main thing, or the head of a school. The principal reason...
  • Principle: a rule or belief. It is a matter of principle.

accept and except

  • Accept: to receive or agree. She accepted the offer.
  • Except: excluding. Everyone except Tom arrived.

fewer and less

  • Fewer is used with countable nouns: fewer apples.
  • Less is used with uncountable nouns: less water.

Strategy for exams

Read your writing aloud in your head. Substitute the longer form ("it is", "they are") to test contractions. If it makes sense, use the contraction.

Key vocabulary

  • Affect vs effectAffect is usually a verb meaning to influence; effect is usually a noun meaning a result.
  • Their, there, they'reTheir shows possession, there shows place or existence, and they're means they are.
  • Its vs it'sIts shows possession; it's means it is or it has.
  • Then vs thanThen refers to time or sequence; than is used in comparisons.
