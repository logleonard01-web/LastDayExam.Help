Algebra uses letters to represent numbers so that you can describe relationships and solve problems in general. Most algebra errors come from a few repeated slips, not from the big ideas. Revising well means practising the core skills and checking for those slips.

## Key concepts

A [variable](/vocabulary/variable/) is a symbol, usually a letter, standing for a number. An [expression](/vocabulary/expression/) combines numbers and variables with operations, such as 3x + 5. An [equation](/vocabulary/equation/) states that two expressions are equal, such as 3x + 5 = 20. To solve an equation is to find the value of the variable that makes it true.

A [coefficient](/vocabulary/coefficient/) is the number multiplying a variable. In 3x + 5, the coefficient of x is 3.

## The skills to revise, in order

1. **Simplifying expressions.** Collect like terms: 4x + 3x = 7x, but 4x + 3 cannot be simplified further.
2. **Expanding brackets.** a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply every term inside the bracket. For example, 3(x − 4) = 3x − 12.
3. **Factorising.** The reverse of expanding. 6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3).
4. **Solving linear equations.** See [solving linear equations step by step](/blog/solving-linear-equations/).
5. **Solving quadratic equations.** See [solving quadratic equations](/blog/solving-quadratic-equations/).
6. **Rearranging formulas.** Treat the target letter as the unknown and undo operations in reverse order.
7. **Inequalities.** Solve like equations, but reverse the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

## Worked example: rearranging a formula

Make *r* the subject of C = 2πr.

- Divide both sides by 2π.
- r = C ÷ (2π).

## Worked example: an inequality

Solve −2x + 3 < 11.

- Subtract 3: −2x < 8.
- Divide by −2 and reverse the sign: x > −4.

Check with x = 0, which should work: −2(0) + 3 = 3, and 3 < 11. True.

## Common mistakes

- **Sign errors.** −(x − 3) = −x + 3, not −x − 3.
- **Doing something to only one side** of an equation.
- **Forgetting to multiply every term** when expanding.
- **Treating (a + b)² as a² + b².** In fact (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b².
- **Not checking the answer** by substituting it back.

## How to practise

Do a small set of questions on one skill, check each answer, and write down which kind of error you made. Move on only when you can do a set of five without errors. Use the [mathematics practice questions](/practice/mathematics/).

## Quick recap

Know the vocabulary, simplify and expand carefully, solve equations by doing the same thing to both sides, reverse the sign for negative multipliers in inequalities, and check by substitution.

=== fractions-decimals-percentages
title: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages Compared
subject: mathematics
kind: Supporting explainer
description: How the three forms relate, how to convert between them, and how to compare values.
vocab: fraction, ratio, percentage
related: how-to-revise-algebra
practice: mathematics
---
Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways to write the same kind of quantity: part of a whole.

## Converting between forms

| From | To | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraction | Decimal | Divide numerator by denominator | 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 |
| Decimal | Percentage | Multiply by 100 | 0.75 → 75% |
| Percentage | Decimal | Divide by 100 | 40% → 0.40 |
| Percentage | Fraction | Write over 100, then simplify | 40% = 40/100 = 2/5 |
| Decimal | Fraction | Use place value, then simplify | 0.6 = 6/10 = 3/5 |

## Useful equivalents to know

- 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%
- 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%
- 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%
- 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%
- 1/10 = 0.1 = 10%
- 1/3 = 0.333... (repeating) ≈ 33.3%

## Finding a percentage of an amount

To find 15% of 60: 10% of 60 is 6, 5% is 3, so 15% is 9. Or multiply: 0.15 × 60 = 9.

## Percentage change

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

**Example.** A price rises from 80 to 92.
- Change: 92 − 80 = 12.
- 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15.
- Increase of 15%.

## Comparing values

To compare 3/8, 0.4 and 35%, convert all to decimals: 3/8 = 0.375, 0.4, and 35% = 0.35. In order from smallest: 35%, 3/8, 0.4.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing the wrong way round when converting a fraction (use numerator ÷ denominator).
- Using the new value instead of the original in percentage change.
- Adding percentages from different bases. A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall does not return to the start: 100 → 110 → 99.

## Quick recap

Think of the three forms as interchangeable. Memorise common equivalents, and for percentage change always divide by the original value.

=== solving-linear-equations
title: Solving Linear Equations Step by Step
subject: mathematics
kind: Worked example
description: A clear method for solving equations with one unknown, with worked examples and checks.
vocab: equation, variable, coefficient
related: how-to-revise-algebra, solving-quadratic-equations
practice: mathematics
---
A linear equation has a variable raised only to the power one. The method is to do the same operation to both sides until the variable is alone.

## The method

1. Expand brackets if there are any.
2. Collect variable terms on one side and numbers on the other.
3. Simplify.
4. Divide by the coefficient of the variable.
5. Check by substituting.

## Example 1: two steps

Solve 3x + 5 = 20.

- Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15.
- Divide both sides by 3: x = 5.
- Check: 3(5) + 5 = 20. ✓

## Example 2: variable on both sides

Solve 5x − 4 = 2x + 11.

- Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x − 4 = 11.
- Add 4 to both sides: 3x = 15.
- Divide by 3: x = 5.
- Check: left 5(5) − 4 = 21, right 2(5) + 11 = 21. ✓

## Example 3: with brackets

Solve 2(x + 3) = 14.

- Expand: 2x + 6 = 14.
- Subtract 6: 2x = 8.
- x = 4.
- Check: 2(4 + 3) = 14. ✓

## Example 4: with fractions

Solve x/3 + 2 = 6.

- Subtract 2: x/3 = 4.
- Multiply by 3: x = 12.
- Check: 12/3 + 2 = 6. ✓

## Common mistakes

- Changing the sign wrongly when moving a term. Instead of "moving", think "subtract the same thing from both sides".
- Forgetting to multiply every term when clearing a fraction.
- Skipping the check, which catches most arithmetic slips.

## Quick recap

Expand, collect, simplify, divide, check.

=== solving-quadratic-equations
title: Solving Quadratic Equations
subject: mathematics
kind: Supporting explainer
description: Three methods for solving quadratics: factorising, the quadratic formula, and completing the square.
vocab: quadratic-equation, factor, root
related: how-to-revise-algebra, solving-linear-equations
practice: mathematics
---
A [quadratic equation](/vocabulary/quadratic-equation/) can be written as ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero. Its solutions are called [roots](/vocabulary/root/).

## Method 1: factorising

Use this when the equation factorises neatly.

**Example.** Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0.

- Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5: 2 and 3.
- (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0.
- Either x + 2 = 0 or x + 3 = 0, so x = −2 or x = −3.
- Check x = −2: 4 − 10 + 6 = 0. ✓ Check x = −3: 9 − 15 + 6 = 0. ✓

## Method 2: the quadratic formula

For ax² + bx + c = 0:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ (2a)

**Example.** Solve 2x² − 3x − 2 = 0, so a = 2, b = −3, c = −2.

- b² − 4ac = 9 − 4(2)(−2) = 9 + 16 = 25.
- √25 = 5.
- x = (3 ± 5) ÷ 4.
- x = 8/4 = 2 or x = −2/4 = −0.5.
- Check x = 2: 8 − 6 − 2 = 0. ✓ Check x = −0.5: 0.5 + 1.5 − 2 = 0. ✓

## The discriminant

The value b² − 4ac tells you how many real roots there are:

- Greater than 0: two different real roots.
- Equal to 0: one repeated root.
- Less than 0: no real roots.

## Method 3: completing the square

Rewrite x² + bx as (x + b/2)² − (b/2)². This method also gives the vertex of the parabola and is how the quadratic formula is derived.

**Example.** Solve x² + 6x + 2 = 0.

- (x + 3)² − 9 + 2 = 0.
- (x + 3)² = 7.
- x = −3 ± √7, approximately −0.35 or −5.65.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to set the equation equal to zero before factorising.
- Losing the sign of b. Write b with its sign inside brackets when substituting.
- Forgetting the ± and giving only one solution.
- Dividing both sides by x, which can lose a solution.

## Quick recap

Rearrange to equal zero. Try factorising. If that fails, use the formula. Check by substitution.

=== area-and-perimeter-formulas
title: Area, Perimeter and Volume Formulas
subject: mathematics

vocab: area, perimeter, volume, radius
related: pythagorean-theorem

---
## Key concept

**Perimeter** is the distance around a 2D shape. **Area** is the amount of surface it covers. **Volume** is the space inside a 3D shape. Perimeter is measured in units of length (cm, m), area in square units (cm², m²), and volume in cubic units (cm³, m³).

## Formulas

| Shape | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle (length l, width w) | 2l + 2w | l × w |
| Square (side s) | 4s | s² |
| Triangle (base b, height h) | sum of sides | ½ × b × h |
| Parallelogram | sum of sides | b × h |
| Trapezium (parallel sides a, b, height h) | sum of sides | ½ × (a + b) × h |
| Circle (radius r) | circumference = 2πr | πr² |

| Solid | Volume |
|---|---|
| Cuboid | l × w × h |
| Cylinder (radius r, height h) | πr²h |
| Cone | ⅓ × πr²h |
| Sphere | (4/3)πr³ |
| Prism | cross-section area × length |

## Worked example 1: circle

A circle has radius 5 cm. Find its circumference and area, using π ≈ 3.14.

- Circumference = 2 × 3.14 × 5 = 31.4 cm.
- Area = 3.14 × 5² = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5 cm².

## Worked example 2: cylinder

A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Use π ≈ 3.14.

- Volume = 3.14 × 3² × 10 = 3.14 × 9 × 10 = 282.6 cm³.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing units, such as cm with m.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius. The radius is half the diameter.
- Using the slant height instead of the perpendicular height in a triangle.
- Writing area in cm instead of cm².

## Quick recap

Learn the table, check units, and identify the radius before substituting.

=== pythagorean-theorem
title: The Pythagorean Theorem
subject: mathematics
kind: Supporting explainer
description: What the theorem says, when it applies, and how to use it to find missing sides.
vocab: hypotenuse, right-angle
related: area-and-perimeter-formulas
practice: mathematics
---
In a right-angled triangle, the square of the [hypotenuse](/vocabulary/hypotenuse/) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides:

a² + b² = c²

where c is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle and the longest side.

## When to use it

Only for right-angled triangles. If a triangle has no right angle, you need other tools such as the sine or cosine rule.

## Finding the hypotenuse

**Example.** The shorter sides are 6 and 8.

- c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100.
- c = √100 = 10.

## Finding a shorter side

**Example.** The hypotenuse is 13 and one side is 5.

- 5² + b² = 13², so 25 + b² = 169.
- b² = 144.
- b = 12.

## Pythagorean triples

Sets of whole numbers that satisfy the theorem include 3, 4, 5; 5, 12, 13; and 8, 15, 17. Multiples of these, such as 6, 8, 10, also work.

## Checking whether a triangle is right-angled

Sides 7, 24, 25: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25². Yes, it is a right-angled triangle.

## Common mistakes

- Adding the wrong sides. The hypotenuse is on one side of the equation alone.
- Forgetting to take the square root at the end.
- Using it for non-right triangles.

## Quick recap

a² + b² = c² for right triangles. Identify the hypotenuse first, substitute, and take the square root.

=== mean-median-mode
title: Mean, Median, Mode and Range
subject: mathematics
kind: Supporting explainer
description: How to calculate and choose between the common measures of average and spread.
vocab: mean, median, mode, range
related: probability-basics
practice: mathematics
---
Averages summarise a set of numbers with one value. Each type of average answers a different question.

## Definitions

- **[Mean](/vocabulary/mean/):** add all values, then divide by how many there are.
- **[Median](/vocabulary/median/):** the middle value when the data is in order. If there are two middle values, take their mean.
- **[Mode](/vocabulary/mode/):** the most frequent value.
- **[Range](/vocabulary/range/):** largest value minus smallest value.

## Worked example

Data: 4, 7, 7, 9, 13.

- Mean: (4 + 7 + 7 + 9 + 13) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = 8.
- Median: the middle of five ordered values is the third, 7.
- Mode: 7 appears twice, so the mode is 7.
- Range: 13 − 4 = 9.

## Even number of values

Data: 2, 5, 6, 10.

- Median: the middle two values are 5 and 6, so the median is (5 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.5.

## Which average to use

- Use the **mean** when values are fairly symmetric and no extreme values distort the data.
- Use the **median** when there are extreme values (outliers). For example, in incomes, a few very high values can raise the mean while the median stays typical.
- Use the **mode** for categories, such as the most common shoe size.

## Effect of an outlier

Data: 3, 4, 4, 5, 40.

- Mean = 56 ÷ 5 = 11.2, which is larger than four of the five values.
- Median = 4, which describes the typical value better.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to order the data before finding the median.
- Dividing by the wrong number when finding the mean.
- Saying "no mode" when more than one value is tied. A data set can have more than one mode.

## Quick recap

Mean is the balance point, median is the middle, mode is the most common, and range measures spread.

=== probability-basics
title: Probability Basics
subject: mathematics

vocab: probability, outcome, event
related: mean-median-mode
practice: mathematics
---
[Probability](/vocabulary/probability/) measures how likely an event is, on a scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). It can be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage.

## The basic formula

When all outcomes are equally likely:

P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes.

**Example.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. The probability of rolling an even number is 3/6 = 1/2, because 2, 4 and 6 are favourable out of six outcomes.

## Complementary events

P(not A) = 1 − P(A).

If the probability of rain is 0.3, the probability of no rain is 0.7.

## Independent events

Two events are independent if one does not affect the other. For independent events:

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

**Example.** Toss a fair coin and roll a fair die. P(heads and a 6) = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.

## Mutually exclusive events

Events that cannot happen together are mutually exclusive. Then:

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).

**Example.** Roll a die. P(1 or 2) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3.

## Without replacement

A bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. Two are drawn without putting the first back. P(both red):

- First red: 3/5.
- Second red: 2/4 = 1/2.
- Both: 3/5 × 1/2 = 3/10.

## Common mistakes

- Adding probabilities for events that are not mutually exclusive.
- Forgetting that the total changes when items are not replaced.
- Giving a probability greater than 1 or less than 0.
- Thinking that after several heads, tails is "due". For a fair coin, each toss is independent.

## Quick recap

Count favourable over total, use 1 − P(A) for "not", multiply for independent "and", add for mutually exclusive "or".
